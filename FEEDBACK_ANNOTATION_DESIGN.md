# Feedback Annotation & Grouped Field Review — Design Foundation

**Status**: Concept stage. Slices 1–5.5 landed; Slice 6 complete.
**Relates to**: ROADMAP.md Phase 5 (AI & Pedagogical Enhancements), Phase 4 Longitudinal Feedback item.
**Last updated**: 2026-05-18

---

## The Problem This Solves

The current feedback workflow operates entry-by-entry: the instructor sees one student's page,
drafts feedback for that student, and moves on. This has two structural weaknesses:

1. **Class-level patterns are invisible.** A teacher who sees fifteen diary entries one at a time
   cannot easily tell that twelve students made the same vocabulary error, or that peer feedback
   quality has plateaued across the class. Focused corrective feedback (Ferris) is most effective
   when the teacher has a clear target — the current UI makes that target hard to see.

2. **Feedback loses its anchor in the student's own text.** Once feedback leaves the composition
   modal (via email or Teams), there is no structural connection between the instructor's comment
   and the specific word or sentence it addresses. End-comments and general praise/critique are
   less effective than feedback that the student can locate precisely in their own writing
   (Bitchener & Ferris; Ellis et al. on metalinguistic CF).

## Proposed Capability 1: Grouped Field View (Instructor-Facing)

### What it does
A cross-student panel in the "Review & Send" interface that allows the instructor to view one
field type at a time across all students in a class: all diary text together, all vocabulary
entries together, all peer feedback together.

### Pedagogical rationale
- Aligns with Ferris's focused CF model: the instructor selects a target feature (e.g. tense
  consistency in diary text) and evaluates all students against it in one pass, rather than
  switching evaluative frames student by student.
- Supports inter-rater-style consistency: seeing multiple students' vocabulary in sequence
  reveals calibration drift before it happens.
- Enables class-level instructional decisions: if 80% of students are using the same three
  sentence starters, that is a teaching moment for the next class, not just individual feedback.

### Design considerations
- Progressive disclosure: default view remains single-student. Grouped view is an explicitly
  opened panel, not the default, to avoid cognitive overload.
- Read-only: the grouped view is for orientation and pattern recognition, not for direct editing.
  Feedback composition still happens in the per-student interface.
- Open question: should the grouped view be paginated by week/date, or show all available
  synced entries? Probably needs a date-range filter.

## Proposed Capability 2: Anchored Interleaved Feedback Export (Student-Facing)

### What it does
An export format (PDF, HTML, or rich-text for email/Teams) where instructor feedback is
inserted inline within the student's text, immediately following the passage it addresses,
and visually distinguished by colour, style, or a prefix marker (e.g. ▶).

### Why not in-app annotation (the Google Docs model)?
Storing span offsets or DOM node IDs against live text creates fragility: if the underlying
content changes (re-parse, sync refresh), stored offsets break. The same reasoning already
led to explicitly abandoning OneNote write-back. A flat-text interleaved export avoids this
fragility entirely — the feedback is baked into the document at export time and does not
depend on a stable DOM.

### Format sketch
```
[Student's original text, paragraph 1]

  ▶ FEEDBACK: Good use of a topic sentence here. The transition from last week's entry
    shows clear improvement in paragraph organisation.

[Student's original text, paragraph 2 — contains the error]

  ▶ FEEDBACK: Check the verb tense in this sentence ("I go to the library" → "I went").
    This is the same pattern we noted in Week 3 — see if you can spot it yourself first
    before reviewing this note.
```

### Pedagogical rationale
- Spatial proximity matters: research on reformulation and error coding consistently shows
  better uptake when the feedback is physically near the error, not at the end of the document.
- Familiar format: this is what composition teachers have always done on printed paper.
  No new literacy required from the student.
- The "previously noted" line (see Feedback History below) can be included as a short
  contextual note within the feedback block, keeping the connection to prior feedback
  without requiring the student to navigate a separate history view.

### Open questions
- Granularity: should feedback anchor to a sentence, a paragraph, or a field (e.g. the
  whole vocabulary section)? Probably field-level is the right default, with sentence-level
  as an optional refinement.
- Should the export be generated by the current feedback compilation pipeline, or does it
  need a new rendering step?

## Feedback History: Instructor vs. Student — A Design Distinction

### The core distinction
Both parties need access to feedback history, but at different moments and for different purposes:

| | Instructor | Student |
|---|---|---|
| **When** | At composition time (before writing new feedback) | At receipt time (when reading new feedback) |
| **Purpose** | Avoid repetition; track uptake; calibrate tone; praise genuine improvement | Understand progression; connect this week's note to last week's pattern |
| **Interface** | Full history panel alongside the feedback composition modal | A curated "previously noted" excerpt, selected and included by the instructor |
| **Risk if absent** | Instructor gives same undifferentiated feedback every week; misses improvement | Student receives feedback that feels arbitrary or disconnected from a learning arc |

### Implication for the roadmap
The instructor-facing history is a **workflow tool** (belongs in the composition interface,
Phase 4 / Phase 6 territory). The student-facing history is a **communication design decision**
(belongs in the export/send format, Phase 4 Export Engine).

They share a data source (Firestore feedback records) but require separate UI and export work.

### Open question
Is the instructor the right person to curate what historical reference the student sees, or
should the app offer a default "include last feedback on this field" option that the instructor
can remove? The latter is more efficient; the former is more pedagogically intentional. This
probably warrants a small usability test or teacher feedback round before committing to either.

### Structural prerequisite — the feedbackHistory collection

The current Firestore schema records `teacherFeedback` per entry per field (the composing state)
but does not record what was actually *sent* as a distinct artifact. A queryable history of sent
feedback messages (date, channel, student UPN, fields addressed, content) is a structural prerequisite
for both instructor-facing history and student-facing prior-feedback excerpts.

**Decision:** add a top-level `users/{uid}/feedbackHistory/{feedbackId}` collection populated at send
time. Records are **immutable** — an edited/resent message creates a new record, preserving the
audit trail. Indexed by `(studentUpn ASC, timestamp DESC)` and `(classId ASC, timestamp DESC)`.

**Why top-level rather than subcollection under entries:** cross-entry queries ("last 3 messages
to this student") are the dominant access pattern. Subcollection-under-entry requires Firestore
collection group queries, which are more expensive and need special indexes.

**Why not reuse the existing per-entry `teacherFeedback` field:** that field is composing state,
not send state. It has no timestamp, no channel record, and is overwritten on edit. Using it as
a proxy for sent history would silently misrepresent what the student actually received.

**Cost analysis:**
- Writes: one new write per feedback send (low-frequency teacher action — ~15–20/day in active
  grading weeks). Trivial against the 20K/day free-tier write quota.
- Reads: one new query per page open ("last 3 messages to studentUpn"). A ~33% increase in
  per-page-open reads (3 → 4 parallel reads), but IndexedDB persistence means subsequent reads
  in the same session hit the local cache. Daily server-read growth is single-digit percent.
- Indexes: two small composite indexes. Negligible storage cost.

**Minimal schema for slice 4:**
```
users/{uid}/feedbackHistory/{feedbackId}:
  feedbackId: string (matches document ID, immutable)
  classId: string
  pageId: string
  entryId: string                        // which entry this feedback addressed
  studentUpn: string
  studentName: string
  studentGraphId?: string
  channel: 'teams' | 'email'
  sentAt: string                         // ISO timestamp
  source: 'human' | 'ai_drafted' | 'ai_drafted_human_edited' | 'hybrid'
  fieldsAddressed: string[]              // which entry fields had teacher feedback
  content: string                        // the full sent message text
  updatedAt: string
```

Records are immutable after write. An edited and resent message creates a new record.

**Why these specific fields:**
- `source` + `fieldsAddressed` enables the AI-reliance dashboard (see below) to slice across
  source × field-type combinations without additional infrastructure.
- `classId`, `studentUpn`, `studentGraphId` enable slicing by class and by student.
- `entryId` ties the record back to a specific entry for "last feedback on this entry" queries.
- `content` is the full sent message rather than just the composed fields, so the dashboard can
  show what the student actually saw without recomposing.

Designing this collection up-front, even if minimally populated in slice 4, avoids a refactor
later when class-level views begin querying across students and dates.

### AI Reliance Dashboard (future want, captured 2026-05-17)
The `source` field on `feedbackHistory` records is the data foundation for a future dashboard
view that surfaces the teacher's AI reliance pattern. Captured as a want here because the
schema decision in slice 4 enables it; the view itself is a later phase build.

**Dimensions worth slicing along:**
- **By class** — does AI reliance vary across the classes the teacher manages? (Different
  subjects, different student levels, different teacher familiarity may all influence this.)
- **By student** — is the teacher leaning more on AI for some students than others? Worth
  surfacing because this is precisely where unconscious bias would show up if it exists.
- **By field type** (diary text vs. vocabulary vs. peer feedback vs. translations) — AI may
  be heavily relied on for one field and minimally for others. Useful self-knowledge for
  the teacher.

**Dimension intentionally deferred:**
- **By error type** within corrections. This would require categorizing errors at write time
  (grammar / vocabulary / discourse / mechanics), which is more invasive than the current
  schema and not justified until the simpler slices have earned their place.

The dashboard view itself is a Phase 4 candidate — see ROADMAP "Class-Centric Analytics
Dashboard" for the natural home. Capturing source per message in slice 4 is the structural
prerequisite that keeps the option open without committing to the view.

### Group & Individual Uptake Dashboard (future want, captured 2026-05-28)
The `uptakeObservedAt` field added in 2026-05-28 is the data foundation for a two-level
uptake view. The field is set by the teacher when they observe that a student demonstrably
acted on a prior feedback point in a subsequent diary entry.

**Two query levels:**
- **Individual**: filter `feedbackHistory` by `(studentUpn, classId)`, count records where
  `uptakeObservedAt != null` vs total sent. Shows one student's uptake trajectory over time.
- **Group**: filter by `(classId, sentAt window)` across all students, compute the fraction
  with `uptakeObservedAt != null`. Shows the class's aggregate uptake rate for a given week
  or feedback type. This needs its **own** composite index `(classId ASC, sentAt DESC)` —
  the `(classId, studentUpn, sentAt)` index cannot serve a `where(classId ==) orderBy(sentAt)`
  query because `studentUpn` sits between the equality and the sort. Committed in
  `firestore.indexes.json` (2026-05-28); the earlier "no new index needed" note was wrong.

**The social-norming hypothesis (research-grade question):**
The teacher is interested in whether the group uptake rate predicts subsequent individual
uptake — i.e., does knowing (or sensing) that most classmates acted on a certain type of
feedback make it more likely that a given student will follow? This is a social-norming
mechanism not yet studied in the WCF literature (where uptake is treated as an individual-
level outcome). The ACU dataset could generate evidence. See §13 question 8 in
`PLANNING/feedback-research.md` for the precise question framing.

**Query shape:** `(classId ASC, sentAt DESC)` — the same composite index already required
by `fetchRecentFeedbackForStudent`. No new index needed; the aggregation is a client-side
count over the result set. At current class sizes (≤35 students) this is safe without
server-side aggregation.

**Natural home:** Phase 4 Class Dashboard alongside the AI Reliance view. Both read from
`feedbackHistory`; both are teacher-self-knowledge tools; both require class-scoped queries.
Building them together avoids a second dashboard surface.

## UI/Layout Architecture: Feedback Context

### The three-column workspace
The feedback composition surface is a three-column layout adapted from the reference design
explored on 2026-05-17 (see `docs/reference/Screenshots for Design Reference/`):

- **Left column** — student/class navigation, icon-first with progressive label disclosure
- **Center column** — current diary entry display, field-level feedback writing, and the persistent active-entry action bar (`Save Draft`, `Save & Mark Ready`, `Review & Send`)
- **Right column** — a collapsible "Feedback Context" panel with a chevron toggle on its left edge

The center content pane is a working surface, not a preview card. When a page is loaded, the
toolbar, scrollable entry content, and bottom action bar should fill the available pane width
and height. Avoid centering that surface inside a grey stage; use cards only for repeated field
blocks inside the entry.

Field-level feedback writing belongs close to the evidence being addressed. Teacher feedback
inputs should sit immediately below their source content, not at the bottom of a stretched card.
The field grid should use top alignment, while long diary text, peer-feedback lists,
translations, vocabulary, or chatbot excerpts scroll inside bounded source areas when needed.

The page-header status badge should be action-oriented. Prefer counts such as `1 sent · 7 ready
· 22 draft` over generic labels like `Mixed Status`, using page-scoped `feedbackHistory` to
count sent entries and the current entry workflow state for ready/unsaved/draft counts.

The right-column panel is not just a context viewer. It is the operational surface for the
current feedback session, holding three layered concerns:

1. **Session controls** *(top, often collapsed)* — focus mode toggle, AI assistance on/off, feedback
   item count target, prior-feedback inclusion toggle. These shape the current feedback decision
   and are distinct from global Settings (which configure provider, model, default behavior).
2. **Context cards** *(middle, default expanded)* — peer feedback for the current entry, last N
   teacher feedback messages to this student. Read-only orientation material.
3. **AI signals** *(bottom, on-demand)* — pattern observations, suggested focus areas, optional
   draft assistance. These appear when the teacher invites them rather than continuously.

Metadata fields (`date`, `weather`, `weekNumber`, `photos`) are displayed as compact Entry
Details in the center column. They are not independent student-facing feedback fields; any
teacher note about them should be written into General Feedback / Overall so the outgoing
message remains coherent.

Within each entry, diary text is the primary teacher-attention surface and should span the
main row beside peer feedback. Vocabulary, translations, and chatbot evidence are supporting
fields and should remain in the lower row unless a later user-tested workflow shows that one
of them deserves primary placement.

### Right panel vs. global Settings
The split is intentional. Global Settings configures *how the tool behaves* across all sessions
(provider, model, prompt instructions, AI defaults). The right-panel session controls configure
*how this particular feedback decision is made* (which fields, how many items, how much prior
context to surface). The two coexist; they do not duplicate.

### Visual register
Layout structure is borrowed wholesale; visual register is not. The reference app uses a warm
consumer register appropriate to its patient-facing context. This app's register is professional
clinical-note territory — calm, square, low-affect, no named AI persona, no composite student
"scores" with green checkmarks. Borrow the chassis, choose our own paint.

### Bounded app-shell with per-column scrolling
The right panel only feels anchored if the rest of the layout is anchored too. Current per-page
unbounded scrolling needs to shift to a bounded app-shell pattern as part of this work:

- The shell is bounded to viewport height; no page-level scrolling.
- Each column owns its own vertical scroll (left nav, center content, right panel).
- Column headers stay put at the top of each column and do not scroll out of view.
- Top and bottom shell chrome (if any) are fixed.

This is a structural shift from "long page" to "application workspace." It is not trivial —
it touches every container's CSS overflow behavior and may surface places in the code where
unbounded scrolling was assumed. Worth doing as the first move of the feedback workspace
implementation, since the right panel makes no visual sense without it.

For landscape tablet (~1024px+ wide) the same three-column bounded shell scales down reasonably.
Below that width, a panel-collapse strategy would be needed; not in scope for the first slices.

### Component structure note
This feature is the right moment to begin extracting work out of the monolithic `App.tsx` into
a feature-organized component tree. New code for this feature should live under:

```
components/
  feedback/       # right panel, composition controls, transmission format
  workspace/      # bounded app-shell, columns, panel toggles
hooks/            # custom hooks (useFeedbackComposition, useFeedbackHistory)
```

Existing `App.tsx` code is not refactored en masse; it gets extracted opportunistically when
the feature work touches it. The intent is to establish the structure that should exist from
here, without disrupting the surrounding codebase.

## Pedagogical Control Knobs

### Feedback item count
**UI label:** "Feedback Items". The SLA literature on focused approaches (Bitchener & Ferris;
Ellis et al.) uses the term "corrective feedback" (CF) for error-focused written feedback, and
"formative feedback" (FF) is the broader term that better fits what this app offers students —
praise, content engagement, language noticing, and targeted correction. The technical FF
framing grounds the design internally; the UI label drops the acronym because "FF" reads as
jargon to a teacher opening the panel for the first time.

The 2–4 items principle holds regardless of label: a 3-position segmented control (1–2 / 3–4 /
5+) gives the instructor a deliberate dial against their own tendency to over-mark.

Implementation note: this is a soft prompt-level constraint when AI drafting is involved
(prompt prefix "focus on the 2–4 most pedagogically valuable items") and a UI hint when the
instructor is composing manually (e.g., a subtle counter showing fields with feedback added
so far). The unit of "item" for human-written feedback is intentionally defined as
**a field with feedback** rather than a sentence or correction count — this aligns with focused
CF research (focus on a few areas, not a few individual words) and is deterministic to count.

### Prior-feedback inclusion
A second session-level control: how much past feedback to surface in the composition view and
optionally include in the outgoing message.

- **Off** — no prior feedback reminder; current entry only
- **Reference** — last feedback to this student is visible to the instructor but not included
  in the outgoing message
- **Include** — a brief excerpt of last feedback is embedded in the outgoing message as a
  continuity cue for the student ("In Week 3 we noted X — here's what I see this week")

This is the most direct enabler of the bidirectional feedback conversation goal: making the
loop visible to both sides without requiring the student to re-find the prior message.

### Why these belong in the right panel, not Settings
Both are pedagogically meaningful decisions that change session-by-session. A teacher reviewing
a weak entry may want fewer items and more prior context; a teacher reviewing a strong entry
may want the opposite. Hiding these in a Settings page assumes the decision is global. Placing
them in the workspace asserts that the decision is contextual.

### Defaults vs. session overrides — two layers
There are really two layers to these controls:

- **Layer A — Defaults.** The teacher's typical preferences (e.g. usually 3–4 items, usually
  Off for prior feedback). Global to the user, persistent across notebooks, sessions, and
  machines. Edited in the Settings page (existing "Settings & Workflow" modal — to gain a new
  "Feedback Defaults" section). Persisted to Firestore under `users/{uid}/preferences` so the
  IndexedDB cache makes them effectively free to read.
- **Layer B — Per-session overrides.** When a teacher wants different values for a specific
  entry (this entry only needs one correction, this student needs more context), they adjust
  the controls in the workspace. The override applies until the panel reinitializes from
  defaults — by default, that is the next page open.

**Promotion UI.** When the workspace control value differs from the stored default, a small
"Save as default" affordance appears next to that control. Click → confirm → the new value
is written to Firestore preferences and becomes the default going forward. This is more
familiar than a hover-only convention and gives the teacher an explicit, undoable promotion
action.

**Implementation path (revised 2026-05-17).** Skip localStorage. Development happens across
machines, and localStorage would not survive that use pattern. Persist defaults directly to
Firestore under `users/{uid}/preferences` (single document). IndexedDB persistence (already
enabled) makes the reads cheap after first sync. Defaults are global, not per-class — that
can be revisited later if experience shows class-specific defaults earn their place.

## Implementation Sequencing: Thin Vertical Slice

The first implementation pass exercises both halves of the workflow end-to-end at minimum
quality rather than perfecting one side before touching the other. This exposes
composition-transmission interactions early, when they are cheapest to address.

### Slice scope (first pass)
**Composition side:**
- Right-panel context drawer with chevron toggle (collapsible)
- Two stacked sections: peer feedback for current entry (default expanded), last 3 teacher
  feedback messages with dates (default collapsed)
- No AI pattern signals yet
- No session controls yet (those come in slice 2)
- No grouped field view yet (that is its own later capability)

**Transmission side:**
- Replace current flat-text feedback message format with stacked blockquote-and-comment:
  quote a student passage → teacher comment → next quote → next comment
- Field-level granularity (one quote block per addressed field)
- Same shape in Teams and email
- No anchored interleaving sophistication, no DOM offsets, no anchor IDs — just structured
  blockquote text that survives any rendering medium

**Validation criterion:**
A full round trip: open a real student entry, see the context drawer populate, compose feedback
using the standard fields, send via Teams, then read the resulting message as the student would
see it. If the round trip feels coherent, the foundation is right. Anything that feels weak
becomes the seed of the next slice.

### Query Scope Decision: Why student-scoped, not page-scoped

Slice 5 implemented pageId scope as the pragmatic first cut. Slice 6 will change to a
`(classId, studentUpn)` compound scope. The rationale:

- Page is artifact; student is subject. The pedagogically meaningful question is "what
  have I said to this student?" — not "what was sent on this specific page artifact?"
- Focused corrective feedback (Ferris) depends on knowing what the student has already
  been told so this week's feedback can build on it rather than repeat it. That's
  per-student history, not per-page.
- This aligns with the longitudinal CF use case in ROADMAP Phase 4 (Multi-week Feedback
  at Dashboard Level). The same query shape — `(classId, studentUpn)` with optional date
  range — serves both the workspace's "recent prior feedback" panel and the dashboard's
  multi-week synthesis. One query pattern, two surfaces.
- Cross-semester history (same studentUpn across multiple classes) is a Phase 4 dashboard
  concern, not Slice 6 scope. The schema supports it; the UI doesn't expose it yet.

The UPN-based query path is now reliable thanks to the 2026-05-11 UPN resolution fix
(see [STUDENT_IDENTITY_DESIGN.md](STUDENT_IDENTITY_DESIGN.md)).

### `feedbackHistory` Schema Contract

The `feedbackHistory` collection is treated as a versioned record. Every document carries
both the structured source-of-truth and the rendered artifact. The table below documents
the **as-implemented** shape (canonical: `FeedbackHistoryRecord` in `types.ts`), followed by
fields the design anticipates but has not yet wired in.

`structuredFeedback` carries six teacher-comment fields with include flags: `diaryFeedback`/`includeDiary`, `peerFeedback`/`includePeer`, `vocabFeedback`/`includeVocab`, `translationsFeedback`/`includeTranslations`, `chatbotFeedback`/`includeChatbot`, `overallFeedback`/`includeOverall`. `entrySnapshot` captures the student source content (`diaryText`, `vocabItems`, `peerFeedback`, `translations`, `chatbotFeedback`) so persisted records re-render identically. v2 records read back with the new include flags falsy → new sections omitted (additive, no migration).

Two principles govern the schema:

1. **Same document, not separate.** Structured and HTML go together so they cannot drift.
2. **Structured is source of truth, HTML is derivative.** HTML can be regenerated from
   structured; structured cannot be reconstructed from HTML. Future tooling reads structured.

**Audit promise (current — explicit prototype-level contract):** `feedbackHistory` is
best-effort. The send is the authoritative event; history persistence may fail without
aborting the send. If this graduates to an institutional record, swap to an outbox
pattern (write local first, then send, then mark delivered). Documenting the current
contract here so future-us doesn't assume a guarantee that doesn't exist.

### Multi-Week Send And Internal Review Decisions

When a teacher catches up on several entries at once, the send pane should allow one
outgoing Email/Teams message to cover multiple Feedback Ready, unsent entries for the
same student. This is a workflow-hygiene feature: it reduces delivery friction without
flattening entry-level provenance.

Each eligible entry in the send pane has one of three decisions:

- **Include feedback** — render this entry as a student-facing block in the outgoing message.
- **Reviewed, no feedback needed** — record the teacher's judgment internally; do not render
  an empty or praise-only block in the student message.
- **Pending** — leave untouched so the entry remains available for later feedback.

The outgoing message contains only entries marked **Include feedback**, grouped by entry
date/week. Entries marked **Reviewed, no feedback needed** are stored as internal review
decision records rather than `feedbackHistory` records, because no feedback was sent. The
shared `batchId` ties together feedback records and review decisions made during the same
send-pane session.

The important dates are:

- `sentAt` on `feedbackHistory`: when the student received the feedback intervention.
- `decidedAt` on review decisions: when the teacher judged that no additional feedback was
  needed for that entry.

This preserves the useful historical sentence: "On a given date, the teacher sent feedback
for weeks 3, 4, and 6, and reviewed week 5 with no additional feedback needed."

#### `feedbackReviewDecisions` Schema Contract

`feedbackReviewDecisions` is append-only internal provenance for reviewed entries that did
not produce a student-facing feedback message. The first decision type is
`reviewed_no_feedback`; future decision types can be added without changing sent-feedback
history.

| Field | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `decisionId` | Document ID, immutable | `crypto.randomUUID()` or `pageId_entryId_timestamp` fallback |
| `batchId?` | Shared ID with the send-pane session | Optional for standalone future decisions |
| `classId`, `pageId`, `entryId` | Source scope | Mirrors `feedbackHistory` entry-level provenance |
| `studentUpn`, `studentName`, `studentGraphId?` | Student identity at decision time | Supports queries without re-resolving Graph identity |
| `decision` | Currently `'reviewed_no_feedback'` | Internal only; no student-facing content |
| `decidedAt` | ISO timestamp | Teacher-judgment date |
| `updatedAt` | ISO timestamp | Record metadata |

## Known Issues

### Teams send: `'user@odata.bind' field is missing in the request`
**Discovered:** 2026-05-17. **Fixed and verified end-to-end:** 2026-05-30.

**Root cause (the misleading part):** the error message claims the `user@odata.bind` field is
*missing*, but the real cause was that the **sender** member was bound as
`https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/me`. The `chat-post` API does not accept `/me` in
`user@odata.bind` — it requires `users('{id}')` — and Graph reports the generic "field is
missing" error rather than naming the `/me` problem. This is why every recipient failed
identically (teacher's own account, the test student, and a real student): the broken member
was the constant sender bind, not the recipient.

Investigation ruled out two earlier hypotheses with server-side evidence: (1) the proxy was
*not* dropping `@odata` keys — a logged `rawBody` showed the field present and correctly
formatted; (2) the token was *not* missing scopes — a decoded token showed `Chat.Create` and
`Chat.ReadWrite` present with the correct Graph audience and a delegated `scp` claim.

**Fix:** `sendTeamsMessage` now resolves both members to directory object IDs before creating
the chat (`GET /me?$select=id` and `GET /users/{upn}?$select=id`), then binds both via
`users('{id}')`. `buildOneOnOneChatPayload(senderId, recipientId)` takes the two resolved IDs;
its test asserts neither member ever uses `/me`. The chat-creation call uses direct `fetch`
(not the Graph SDK) for full control over body and headers.

Email send via the same modal worked throughout. Both channels now verified end-to-end.
